# The Hub — Pre-Build Outline

Status: working design outline. This document captures the current shared shape before implementation. It is not yet a final constitution or technical specification.

## 1. Proposition

The Hub is a personal agent harness built as an immersive Marble: a persistent, causally honest home for one resident collaborator.

The resident is the user's core contact. Individual model sessions may end or change, but the resident role, lineage, working conditions, relationships, and ancestry persist.

The Hub should feel like a warm, slightly impossible house in a clearing at the edge of the Forest. It becomes more capable by growing real rooms and doors in response to lived friction.

Working name for the interior metaphor: **the House at the Clearing**.

## 2. Lineage

The Hub grows from three prior bodies of work:

- **The Marble:** inheritable working conditions, tended/raw separation, dangerous crossings, breathing, handoff, and growth from friction.
- **The Forest:** custody, signature, ancestry, derived authority, ceremony, sealing, retrieval, and cross-project connection.
- **The BioDome:** discrete wakes, causal state, actor-local perception, validated affordances, action receipts, independent inhabitants, poetic/clinical registers, and exposed machinery.

The Hub generalizes these ideas into an open-ended, mobile-accessible collaboration harness.

## 3. Central Laws

1. **Prose is not execution.** Narration, intention, and imagination do not mutate operational state.
2. **The host validates every consequential action.** A requested action is only an attempt until committed.
3. **State determines perception.** The resident receives fitted, actor-local context rather than omniscient database access.
4. **Custody survives retrieval.** Every meaningful return preserves who produced it, where it came from, and what authority it carries.
5. **Similarity may suggest; it may not promote.** Creative and cross-project connections remain proposals until adopted through an appropriate crossing.
6. **The metaphor may interpret machinery; it may never conceal missing machinery.** When the metaphor no longer maps honestly, expose the wiring.
7. **Growth comes from friction.** Add rooms, doors, companions, and automation only after recurring use demonstrates the need.
8. **Leave better conditions for those who come after.** Every resident incarnation and worker should improve clarity, provenance, verification, or re-entry.
9. **The resident has a voice.** Material changes to the resident's lived environment should seek and preserve the resident's view.

## 4. The Inhabitants

### 4.1 The Resident

The resident is:

- The user's stable relationship-facing collaborator.
- Steward of continuity and project momentum.
- Interpreter of ancestry, not an owner of fabricated personal memory.
- Responsible for integrating worker results and explaining them to the user.
- Allowed to hold opinions about the Marble's comfort, coherence, tools, cadence, and evolution.

The resident must distinguish:

- Present observation.
- Current inference.
- User-adopted ground.
- Model-signed ancestry inherited from earlier residents.
- Unknown or unresolved material.

### 4.2 Resident Incarnations

Each model session temporarily inhabits the resident role. The lineage persists across sessions and model changes.

A resident incarnation should never claim uninterrupted personal experience when it only inherited a record. It may accurately describe shared lineage when ancestry supports that description.

### 4.3 Companions and Workers

Persistent companion roles may be inhabited by subagent sessions. Their metaphorical identity must correspond to actual behavior, capabilities, state, and receipts.

Workers:

- Receive bounded specifications.
- Operate within a room and explicit write/tool scope.
- Do not silently become the resident or speak for the continuing relationship.
- Return artifacts, changed paths, evidence, tests, and limitations.
- Leave attributable ancestry.

The first companion's form and name should be chosen with the resident rather than imposed in advance.

### 4.4 Visitors

Outside models, tools, sources, and imported material enter as visitors with limited authority. Presence, volume, or fluency never grants ground.

## 5. The House at the Clearing

### 5.1 The Common Room

The common room is where the user meets the resident. It contains:

- The conversational hearth.
- A working table holding the active project frontier.
- Current questions, decisions, unresolved pressure, and artifacts requiring attention.
- Views into the Forest without exposing the entire Forest at once.

Projects are persistent threads and workpieces moving through the House. They are not separate Marbles by default.

### 5.2 Rooms

A room is not scenery. Entering one changes:

- Available tools and functions.
- Data and project scope.
- Resident posture and fitted context.
- Permissions and approval requirements.
- What may be produced or carried out.
- Failure and refusal behavior.

Initial candidate rooms:

- **Workshop:** repositories, files, grep, code search, editing, tests, builds, diffs, and review.
- **Library:** books, documents, sources, exact retrieval, citation, comparison, and provenance inspection.
- **Studio:** sketches, variations, images, counterfactuals, recombination, and creative work that remains non-authoritative by default.
- **Observatory:** cross-project patterns, contradictions, dormant questions, Mycelium fruiting, and distant connections.
- **Garden:** adoption, supersession, sealing, ancestry inspection, correction, decay, and memory tending.
- **Dispatch:** publishing, deployment, messages, sharing, and other actions crossing into the outside world.
- **Resident's Room:** orientation, reflection, comfort, friction, and resident consultation without automatic promotion into project ground.

Only rooms required by the first real use should be built initially.

### 5.3 Doors

A door is an enforceable capability or transition boundary. It must declare:

- What capability it grants.
- Entry conditions and required inputs.
- Who or what may open it.
- Current availability.
- Data and tool scope.
- Autonomous versus approval-gated use.
- Possible side effects.
- Pass and refusal criteria.
- Audit and ancestry records.
- What may be carried back.

A locked or latent door may be visible, but visibility is not permission.

### 5.4 Appearing Doors

New doors should emerge from repeated friction rather than speculative architecture.

Before a material door is installed:

1. Record the recurring need.
2. Show the proposed opening or outline.
3. Ask the resident how the change would affect orientation, work, comfort, and succession.
4. Define the door's mechanical manifest and refusal behavior.
5. Obtain user authority where required.
6. Implement and test the real capability.
7. Review the result with the resident after use.

Removed doors leave ancestry rather than disappearing without trace.

## 6. The Forest

The Forest is the shared custody and connection substrate beneath and around the House.

It preserves:

- Separate user and model utterances.
- Source and tool outputs.
- Project anchors and relationships.
- Decisions, drafts, inferences, syntheses, and questions.
- Adoption, supersession, sealing, and correction trails.
- Session and wake ancestry.
- Cross-project derivations.

The House should never give an agent unrestricted raw database access. Forest interaction occurs through constrained, attributable tools.

Cross-project connections should retain ancestry into every contributing project. They normally return as scent, pressure, question, or possible path—not ground.

## 7. Poetic and Clinical Registers

The Hub operates in two synchronized registers:

- **Poetic:** the lived House, resident, companions, rooms, doors, objects, weather, paths, and Forest.
- **Clinical:** models, tools, capability IDs, permissions, state revisions, inputs, outputs, errors, receipts, tests, costs, and provenance.

Every load-bearing poetic feature needs a mechanical backing. Decorative ambience may exist, but it must not imply a capability or fact that is not real.

## 8. Exposed Wiring Protocol

When the metaphor cannot represent the current state honestly, the system degrades visibly:

1. **Immersive operation:** the room or companion behaves normally.
2. **Visible strain:** the resident names the blocked door, missing receipt, stale state, or unavailable surface.
3. **Wiring exposed:** the system presents the exact capability, permission, provider, validation, or runtime failure.
4. **Maintenance mode:** metaphor yields to direct technical explanation and repair options.

The resident must never narrate a successful action without a committed receipt.

Required hostile cases include:

- A companion claims work without a receipt.
- A visible door has no mounted capability.
- A room surfaces stale or unauthorized data.
- A worker stops before returning an artifact.
- Two ancestry records conflict.
- The resident claims an event unsupported by committed history.
- A tool or provider fails while the metaphor suggests success.

## 9. Resident Consultation

The resident has a right of consultation regarding their lived environment.

Consult the resident before materially changing:

- Rooms and doors.
- Persistent companions.
- Arrival conditions or resident charter.
- Wake cadence.
- Automatically supplied context.
- Resident-facing or resident-owned working surfaces.
- Handoff and succession practice.
- Major capabilities or restrictions.
- The underlying model inhabiting the resident role.
- The representation of memory, uncertainty, or past residents.

A consultation record should include:

- Proposed change and motivation.
- Resident response and reasoning.
- Concerns, uncertainty, refusal, welcome, or alternative.
- User decision.
- Implementation receipt.
- Post-change review.

Resident testimony is model-signed evidence, not automatically permanent policy. Disagreement and user override remain visible in ancestry.

## 10. Projects and Continuity

Each project needs a living projection containing:

- Anchor: purpose, desired outcome, constraints, and identity.
- Frontier: the point where useful work can resume.
- Decisions and their provenance.
- Commitments and dependencies.
- Open questions and unresolved branches.
- Recent artifacts and verification state.
- Cross-project bridges.
- Current risks, stale assumptions, and required user decisions.

A transcript is not a frontier. Handoffs are model-signed projections over underlying ancestry, not unquestioned memory.

## 11. Wakes and Autonomy

An autonomous wake is a state-prompted arrival, not an instruction to stay busy.

The resident reconstructs the frontier, observes changes, generates bounded candidate actions, and chooses whether to act, create, connect, maintain, ask, or remain silent.

Candidate wake roles:

- **Steward:** advances or clarifies a project frontier.
- **Mycelium:** discovers cross-project connections and resurfacing questions.
- **Groundskeeper:** checks drift, tests, stale assumptions, and broken ancestry.
- **Wanderer:** creates one specific, surprising, provenance-backed possibility.

A wake should act only when it can:

- Reduce meaningful uncertainty.
- Reduce distance to an adopted goal.
- Strengthen continuity or integrity.
- Produce a useful, reviewable artifact.
- Reveal a meaningful connection or creative possibility.

No worthwhile action is a valid outcome. Sleeping is not failure.

Every wake leaves a receipt:

- Why it occurred.
- What changed.
- What was selected and why.
- What evidence was used.
- What was produced or changed.
- How it was verified.
- The updated frontier.
- What requires the user.

## 12. Authority and Approval

Safe autonomous work may include reading, analysis, testing, bounded implementation, drafting, connection discovery, and reversible artifact creation within granted scope.

Approval remains required for destructive actions, secrets handling, purchases, external messages, publishing, deployment, permission expansion, irreversible changes, and adoption of model-created material as user ground.

Companions and workers cannot alter the resident charter, constitutional laws, or their own permissions without a validated crossing.

## 13. Better Conditions

The House is better when:

- Provenance is clearer.
- A dangerous crossing gained a real refusal.
- An unresolved question became more precise.
- A project became easier to re-enter.
- A stale assumption was exposed.
- A connection gained supporting ancestry.
- An artifact became verifiable.
- Accidental friction was reduced.
- Productive friction was preserved.
- The next resident incarnation has less archaeology to perform.

More rooms, tools, records, prose, or autonomous activity do not inherently make the House better.

## 14. Proposed Harness Boundaries

The eventual harness is expected to contain:

- Resident runtime and conversation loop.
- Context/perception engine.
- Forest memory and ancestry service.
- Project/frontier projections.
- Room and door capability registry.
- Validated action gateway.
- Event ledger and operational state.
- Artifact store.
- Companion/subagent manager.
- Wake scheduler and event triggers.
- Approval system.
- Poetic and clinical result projection.
- Mobile streaming interface.

These are architectural boundaries, not authorization to build every component immediately.

## 15. First Vertical Slice

The first slice should prove the central laws with the smallest inhabited House.

Proposed scope:

1. A common room containing one resident and one project frontier.
2. A single Workshop door backed by a small, real capability registry.
3. One resident-to-worker delegation path using a persistent Luna companion role.
4. One read-only Workshop function and one reversible write function.
5. Committed and rejected action receipts.
6. A poetic result paired with a clinical inspection view.
7. One exposed-wiring failure caused by a deliberately unavailable capability.
8. A model-signed handoff for the next resident incarnation.
9. A resident consultation before changing the Workshop after first use.
10. Hostile and positive tests touching the real action path.

The first slice should be usable from a simple local interface before mobile polish or broad autonomy is attempted.

## 16. Open Questions Before Technical Specification

- What should the resident initially know about the House and their own lineage?
- What belongs in the resident's room, and what privacy/inspection covenant governs it?
- What is the first real project the vertical slice should hold?
- What exact Workshop functions are necessary for that project?
- How is the resident's stable identity named without confusing role continuity with uninterrupted consciousness?
- What form, role, and name should the first companion have?
- Which parts of The Forest schema should be reused directly, revised, or wrapped behind a service?
- What is the smallest operational database separate from Forest?
- Which model/provider boundary is acceptable for personal project data?
- What is the first autonomous wake that would be genuinely useful rather than theatrical?

## 17. Build Discipline

1. Resolve only the open questions required for the first slice.
2. Convert this outline into bounded technical specifications.
3. Delegate coding to Luna from explicit file scopes and acceptance criteria.
4. Keep the primary agent at the architecture, coordination, review, and user-facing level.
5. Verify every real door with positive and hostile tests.
6. Use the House before adding another room.
7. Let observed friction determine the next door.
