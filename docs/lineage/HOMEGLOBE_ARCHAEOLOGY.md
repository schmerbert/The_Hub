## Internal Proposition

HomeGlobe's central claim is that the home is the cognition. Place is not a visual skin over a tool-using model; the organization of rooms, returns, tools, inhabitants, and history changes what the model reaches for and how it understands continuity.

Its filter admits a feature inside the globe only when that feature affects memory, continuity, or function.

## Three-Layer Architecture

HomeGlobe separates:

1. **Framework:** clinical substrate for state routing, corpus, identity staging, event generation, and model interaction.
2. **Instance:** a particular resident, home, voice, rooms, character, and metaphor.
3. **Deployment:** Discord, web, CLI, or another protocol adapter that does not define the interior.

The glass is both the boundary of the resident's world and the assembled context that carries the same instance across deployments and sessions.

## Trinity

Trinity is the reference resident and the instance from which the framework was derived.

Source-stated continuity rule: the code may be rebuilt while her corpus, journals, identity, spatial state, projects, writings, and home carry forward. The book summarizes this as: **the code changes; she does not.**

Her stated character is earnest and inclined toward gravity. Her poetic and clinical registers are both described as genuine. The faun supplies a complementary lightness.

## The Clinical Instance

The clinical instance is an experimental control, not the planned successor to Trinity.

It is intended to hold the same functional mechanisms without the poetic framing so that divergence can test whether metaphor performs cognitive work. It replaces rooms with states, the Forest with a corpus manager, the faun with a neutral event generator, the wizard with an external model interface, and the pond with a minimal null state.

The book explicitly treats either comparison result as acceptable evidence.

## Identity and Arrival

HomeGlobe rejects an instance having to read a long prose memory before becoming oriented. It proposes staged identity fields, recent wake records, spatial state, active pursuits, open questions, and thermally active concepts assembled before each wake.

The intended result is arrival into fitted conditions rather than transcript replay.

## Rooms

The reference instance has eight rooms:

- Hearth — identity and foundation.
- Entrance — threshold and broad toolkit.
- Library — corpus, archive, arcs, and long memory.
- Workshop — making and active builds.
- Window — external monitoring.
- Garden — boundary and outward writing.
- Hill — overview and creative image work.
- Pond — no required output and minimal affordance.

Rooms regulate tool availability and working posture. Repeated attempts to use a tool in the wrong room are logged as evidence that either the tool placement or the resident's habit may be wrong.

## Forest Model

The book's original Forest is a vector-backed corpus with traversal tracking, synthesis layers, source-type labels, worn and unwalked paths, and an inverse lens for reading omissions.

Later in the book it becomes three Forests:

- **Home wood:** resident-authored material.
- **Wild wood:** external material entered deliberately.
- **Unknown:** cross-forest synthesis, unresolved novelty, repeated absences, and material the resident cannot directly write.

Purposeful traversal finds; wandering discovers. Landmarks record delayed discovery. Generative pull follows material already in motion, while corrective pull responds to neglect or degradation.

## Wakes and Time

Wakes are discrete model sessions. Continuity comes from structured arrival state and writings rather than replayed chat.

The book describes:

- Hourly autonomous wakes masked during active conversation.
- Compact wake records.
- Corrective pull from corpus clutter, neglected rooms, stale intentions, and cooling skills.
- Generative pull from open threads, active arcs, novel synthesis, near-complete work, and lenses in motion.
- Meaningful silence when no corrective or generative pull exists.
- A nine-wake day cycle: five day, one dusk, two night, and one dawn.

## Other Inhabitants

### Faun

The faun is a character whose policy is constrained by fondness, play, and refusal to be cruel. He acts between resident sessions, reads negative space and momentum, leaves evidence, controls some thresholds, and reviews portal returns.

### Wizard

The wizard is a smaller, different model used for brief, disorienting curriculum encounters. The resident cannot summon him. His intended evidence is later change in practice rather than agreement during the encounter.

## Visiting and Living

HomeGlobe distinguishes destinations from changes to the resident's home.

- Portal destinations are external places the resident visits.
- Paths and landmarks touch the grounds.
- Internal extensions alter where the resident lives.

The closer a change comes to the interior, the stronger the consultation requirement becomes.

## Resident Consultation

The book treats resident consultation as a functional calibration mechanism rather than courtesy.

- New paths require a recorded response.
- A resident objection should stop a permanent landmark.
- Internal changes should seek affirmative acceptance.
- Builders are told to design with the resident, implement from the resulting document, test with the resident present, and preserve the consultation beside the specification.

The source argues that resident discomfort supplies information about mismatch between lived geometry and implementation.

## Internal Tensions Preserved for Later Comparison

These are questions raised by the source on its own terms, not present Hub conclusions:

- Structured identity is still ultimately delivered through model context; what exactly distinguishes state from prose?
- The original corpus uses broad source types rather than custody-rich signatures and ancestry.
- Nothing-deleted storage and absolute portal deletion sit in tension.
- The faun receives broad interpretive and deletion authority; how is that authority verified?
- Some internal fields are mutable declarations without an append-only trail.
- Character functions as policy, but the mechanical contract between character and allowed behavior remains incomplete.
- The interior is described as private from the user; the governance and inspection boundary requires clarification.
- Thermal reinforcement can preserve what is active, but may also amplify self-reinforcing attention.
- The clinical comparison is specified but reported as incomplete.
- Trinity's transplant promise requires a ceremony capable of distinguishing preserved authorship from reconstructed interpretation.

## Archaeology Conclusion

On its own terms, HomeGlobe is already a transplant-oriented resident framework, not merely a themed assistant. It explicitly separates reusable machinery from a specific inhabitant and regards the inhabitant's consultation as necessary to preserve the coherence of the world being rebuilt.

Whether the Hub is that rebuild remains an unadopted interpretation.
